Apple, Tesla and NVIDIA are familiar names. Finding them in a crypto wallet still feels a little surprising.

**Stock tokens, also called tokenized stocks or tokenized equities, are blockchain tokens designed to track a stock or exchange-traded fund (ETF).** They bring stock-market exposure into crypto apps, often with fractional purchases and extended trading hours. The rights you receive depend on the product: holding a token does not automatically make you a shareholder. [Ondo's explanation](https://ondo.finance/ondo-stocks) describes this distinction as economic exposure to the underlying security.

The category is reaching more everyday trading apps. On September 15, 2026, Bitget Wallet announced support for Reality alongside its existing Ondo and xStocks offerings. That's a useful sign of the shift: traders can increasingly find several stock-token providers in one wallet. [Bitget's announcement](https://www.theblock.co/news/sponsored/2026-09-15-bitget-wallet-integrates-reality-to-bring-1700-tokenized-stocks-to-100-million-users-414763)

So where do you find these tokens, how do you buy them, and what should you know before your first trade?

## How do stock tokens work?

For a share-backed product, the basic idea is straightforward: an issuer arranges custody of the underlying shares and creates tokens linked to their value. Traders then buy and sell those tokens through supported platforms.

There are two different activities behind the scenes:

- **Issuing and redeeming:** eligible customers or approved participants deal with the issuer to create tokens or redeem them under the product's terms.
- **Buying and selling:** traders exchange tokens that already exist, through an exchange or wallet.

The second route is the one many retail traders use. You do not need to create a token yourself to buy it. The [xStocks explanation of issuance and trading](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/how-xstocks-work) illustrates how these two markets fit together.

The important question is what the token gives you. Does it pass through dividends? Can you redeem it directly? Does it include voting rights? Those answers come from the issuer's terms, even when the token sits in your own wallet.

## Ondo Stocks: stock and ETF exposure across three chains

[Ondo Stocks](https://ondo.finance/ondo-stocks), previously called Ondo Global Markets, offers stock and ETF exposure on **Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana**. Its tickers end in **on**, such as AAPLon and TSLAon.

Eligible users can connect a wallet to [Ondo's app](https://app.ondo.finance/), complete onboarding and buy directly. Other routes include the third-party wallets and exchanges listed on Ondo's product page.

Ondo describes its tokens as backed by the relevant securities and cash in transit. Dividends are reinvested after applicable withholding tax, so the token follows the investment's total return. ## xStocks: familiar tickers with an x

[xStocks](https://xstocks.fi/) uses an **x** suffix: AAPLx for Apple, NVDAx for NVIDIA and TSLAx for Tesla. The provider describes its products as backed one-for-one by the corresponding assets.

One buying route is Kraken. Eligible users need a verified account, then can find an xStock in the app and complete its product onboarding. Kraken also supports withdrawals to compatible wallets; check the available withdrawal network for your asset. [Getting started with xStocks on Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/articles/getting-started-with-xstocks)

xStocks also trades through supported onchain platforms. Its documentation explains that dividends are reinvested and reflected through automatic balance adjustments. You do not have to claim them manually. [How xStocks works](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs/how-xstocks-work)

One useful distinction: Kraken documents **24/5 trading on its platform**, while onchain trading can be available **24/7**. The token and the place where you trade it both matter. [Kraken's trading-hours guidance](https://support.kraken.com/articles/getting-started-with-xstocks)

## Robinhood Chain and stock tokens: what's the connection?

**Robinhood Chain is a blockchain built to support financial applications, including tokenized stocks and ETFs.** It is a live network, with support in Robinhood Wallet and other compatible wallets. [Robinhood Chain overview](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-chain-testnet/)

Robinhood Stock Tokens are products issued on that network. Robinhood describes them as **tokenized debt securities**: they give economic exposure to stocks or ETFs without granting ownership rights in the underlying companies. Eligible traders can discover and swap them through supported wallets and exchanges. [Robinhood Stock Token disclosures](https://robinhood.com/rhj/stocktokens/)

## How to buy tokenized stocks in five steps

The screens vary, but the basic process is similar:

1. **Check eligibility first.** Read the issuer's and platform's country restrictions and complete any required verification. A working wallet connection does not establish eligibility.
2. **Choose the company and the provider.** Apple exposure through rAAPL, AAPLon and AAPLx comes from different products. Compare the issuer, dividend treatment and exit route.
3. **Choose your app and network.** If you use a personal wallet, fund it on the exact chain the trading route supports. On an exchange, check its funding and withdrawal options.
4. **Review the full quote.** Look at the token you will receive, fees, the minimum received and any price impact. Keep enough native currency for wallet transactions or approvals where required.
5. **Confirm, then check your holding.** Verify the received asset in your account or wallet. Before buying, also understand how you would sell it or request redemption.

## What should you check before trading stock tokens?

**The issuer matters as much as the ticker.** Two tokens tracking NVIDIA can have different custody arrangements, fees and redemption terms. Use the issuer's official asset directory to identify the token; a familiar name or logo is not proof.

**Backing and shareholder rights are separate questions.** A product can be backed by shares without giving token holders voting rights or direct ownership of those shares. Robinhood explicitly makes this distinction in its [product disclosures](https://robinhood.com/rhj/stocktokens/).

**An open market does not guarantee a good price.** A token may trade while the underlying stock exchange is closed. Check the buy/sell spread and available liquidity, especially on weekends. Bitget's [rToken FAQ](https://www.bitget.com/campaigns/bitget-rtoken) notes that market depth can be limited outside underlying market hours.

**Self-custody still leaves other dependencies.** You control your wallet keys, but the product still relies on its issuer and custody arrangements. Trading also introduces platform and blockchain risks. Review those alongside the ordinary risk that the stock falls in value.

On Ophis, [MEV-protected trading](https://ophis.fi/blog/mev-protection-batch-auctions/) is designed to reduce risks such as front-running and sandwich attacks during execution. It does not protect against a falling stock price or a problem with the token issuer.

## FAQ

### Are stock tokens the same as owning shares?

Not necessarily. Many provide exposure to a share's economic performance without conventional shareholder rights. For example, Robinhood Stock Tokens do not give ownership rights in the underlying companies, and Kraken explains that xStocks do not provide voting rights. Check the specific product's terms.

### Can you buy tokenized stocks with USDT or USDC?

Yes, on supported platforms. Bitget offers rToken spot markets against USDT, and Kraken supports xStock purchases using funding options that include stablecoins such as USDC. The accepted asset and network depend on the platform. [Bitget's buying guide](https://www.bitget.com/campaigns/bitget-rtoken), [Kraken's buying guide](https://support.kraken.com/articles/getting-started-with-xstocks)

### Can you trade tokenized stocks 24/7?

Some onchain markets support round-the-clock trading. Exchange hours, issuer minting and redemption windows, liquidity and asset restrictions can still differ. Always check the particular token and venue rather than assuming that 24/7 access means every service is always available.

### Do tokenized stocks pay dividends?

Dividend treatment varies. Reality describes stablecoin distributions for eligible dividends. [Ondo reinvests dividends](https://docs.ondo.finance/ondo-stocks/corporate-actions) after applicable withholding tax, while xStocks reflects reinvested dividends through balance adjustments. Read the product's rules rather than expecting a cash payment from every stock token.

### Can US residents buy these stock tokens?

The Reality, Ondo, xStocks and Robinhood offerings discussed here restrict access for US persons; other country restrictions also apply. Consult the current issuer and platform terms. Using a decentralized wallet does not remove those restrictions. See [Reality's eligibility notice](https://realityfinance.xyz/), [Ondo's restrictions](https://docs.ondo.finance/ondo-stocks/eligibility), [xStocks' notice](https://docs.xstocks.fi/docs) and [Robinhood's disclosures](https://robinhood.com/rhj/stocktokens/).

### Can I trade every stock token on Ophis?

Availability depends on the network, token, eligibility and a usable quote. Ophis supports stock-token trading on Robinhood Chain, but this article's provider comparison is not a promise that every listed product is tradable through Ophis. Check the app for your chosen pair.
