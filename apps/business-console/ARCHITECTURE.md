# Ophis Business console: architecture

Local only. Milestones 1 (sign-in core), 2 (enrolment and recovery ceremony for members, step-up), 3 (roles, two-person approval, freeze rules, audit log page), 4a (wallets, the signing page, the recovery wallet, readiness), 4b (last-Owner recovery), 5a (registration, with its fixes G0 to G8), 6 (Support) and the company sign-in (2026-11-11) are built. 5b (sealing) is not.
Design sources: `security-design.md`, `recovery.md`, `key-custody.md` (owner research folder). The login identifies people. It never signs a trade.

## Configuration
- `ENVIRONMENT` (wrangler var) selects the constants in `passkeys.ts`: relying-party id, origin, the signing page's origin, and two JSON-RPC endpoints from different operators per chain (1, 10, 8453, 42161). None is ever read from a request.
  Today only `local` exists: `localhost`, `http://localhost:8787`, signing page `http://127.0.0.1:8787`, public endpoints (each answered its `eth_chainId` on 2026-11-11). An unknown value fails every call with 500.
- A production entry is added once the hostname is chosen. The relying-party id is the full hostname, and it is final: passkeys are tied to it.
- Secrets (`.dev.vars`, made by `seed`, never printed): `CHALLENGE_KEY` (HMAC key of the stateless challenges and of every sealed text, 32 characters or more), `OPERATOR_TOKEN`, `MICROSOFT_CLIENT_SECRET`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` (the local stand-in takes any value), `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` (Support).
- Company sign-in: `IDPS` in `oidc.ts` holds, per environment, the authorize, token and key-set addresses, the issuer and the client id of each provider. `local` points at the stand-in. `REAL_ENDPOINTS` holds the addresses of Microsoft (`organizations`: work or school accounts only) and Google for a future environment: they have never been called.
- D1 binding `DB`. The real database must be created with `--jurisdiction=eu`. The id in `wrangler.jsonc` is a placeholder.

Audit chain: `hash = SHA-256(JSON([org_id, seq, at, actor_kind, actor_id, session_id, action, subject_id, detail, country, asn, prev_hash]))`; the first `prev_hash` is 64 zeros.
Every state change goes through `commit()` in `data.ts`: the change and its audit row are one `db.batch()`, one transaction.
`commit()` reads the head, then runs the caller's plan, then writes with the next sequence number. Any other write of the organisation in between takes that number and the batch fails, so what a plan read is still true when it commits. One retry.
Rule for new code: whatever decides a write is read inside the plan. A read made by the handler before `commit()` is stale by then (the review found one: a cancelled ticket came back when its link was used at the same instant).

### The ceremony as built (M2; `recovery.md` sections 1 and 2)
- Ticket: 256-bit token, only in the link fragment and in one response; SHA-256 stored. 10 min for key roles, 24 h for Trader and Viewer (a company sign-in link: 24 h for every role). This section is the three-word path, which stays as built for the setting "none"; one text changed: the member sends the words over a channel both trust, and the colleague types them. 3 per issuer and seat per 24 h. Issuer: Owner or Admin, never above the own role, never the own seat. The seat decides the kind: `invited` gives an invitation, `active` a re-enrolment.
- Phrase: `words3(SHA-256("ophis-enrol-v1" ‖ site ‖ org ‖ seat ‖ role ‖ purpose ‖ credentialHash ‖ nonce))`, every field with a 4-byte length; site is the relying-party id, purpose the ticket kind, the nonce 128 server bits that never leave the server. Never stored. Returned once, by `enrol/finish`, to the member's device.
- Confirmation: the issuer types the words within 60 min, in a request that carries its own passkey check (challenge bound to ticket id, credential hash and try number). 3 wrong tries kill the ticket and the pending passkey. Words that are not on the list are a typing slip, not a try.
- Key roles: the issuer's words write an `approvals` row (`ticket.confirm`, requester = issuer). A second key-role member (not issuer, not target) types the words within the same 60 min and becomes its checker. Only where the 30 day rule (M3, below) allows it: `not_before` = 72 h, and the pending passkey becomes active at its first sign-in in the 7 days after. No scheduled job.
- Activation: new passkey `active`; on a re-enrolment the seat's active passkeys become `suspended` with `stop_right_until`, and the seat gets `quarantine_until` (24 h Trader and Viewer, 72 h key roles); the seat's sessions end; its other open tickets die.
- Stop: a suspended passkey with a running stop right opens no session. `signin/finish` tells it so (403 `passkey_replaced`) once its check passed; `stop/finish` then revokes the seat's active passkeys, restores the stopping passkey and the ones replaced with or before it, revokes every passkey added after it was replaced, and freezes the seat. A colleague's freeze does not take the stop right away: on a frozen seat the replaced passkey still stops, until the same date, and the stop also revokes what the freeze had suspended (review of M3: otherwise one Owner could re-enrol a seat, freeze it so that the member cannot stop, and keep the key after an unfreeze).
- A seat is frozen by a stop or by a colleague's freeze (M3). Nobody revokes the last active passkey of a seat, so nobody clears the way for a re-enrolment the member could not stop. A freeze closes, in the tables, the open tickets for that seat and the ones it issued; a frozen seat gets no ticket. A ticket is also dead when read if its issuer is not active.
- Only active passkeys count: toward the two a seat needs and toward the 10 it may hold. Replaced and pending ones do not.
- A sign-in with a passkey the member still holds kills every open ticket of that seat (audit detail `ticket_killed`): this is how the target cancels during the wait.
- Alerts are computed at `GET /api/me` from rows of the last 7 days (no table, no email): quarantine, new passkey on the seat, re-enrolment started or completed, a colleague re-enrolled or joined (not shown to the issuer), a frozen seat (who froze it and when), words or a request waiting for this member, a change two people made (not shown to its approver), and for Owners and Admins a sign-in that closed a re-enrolment ("the issuer is flagged"). The app shows them before the page; the browser remembers what was read.

### Roles, two-person changes and freezes as built (M3; owner decision of 2026-11-11)
- Role table: `routes` in `index.ts` is the table. A row holds the roles (or `public`), `fresh`, `twoKeys`, `settled` (refused in quarantine), `second` (the kind of change) and `can` (its sentence). `api()` enforces the role, then the gates. A `second` route has no handler: all the router can do is file its request. `GET /api/org` returns the rows as `rights`, and the page "What each role can do" renders them.
- Request: an `approvals` row (action = kind, subject, payload `{"value"}`, requester, 7 days). Kinds: `seat.unfreeze`, `seat.remove`, `seat.role`, `policy.synced_keys`. One open request per kind and subject. The requester cancels. A different key-role member rejects, or approves after a fresh check: the change is applied in the commit of the approval (audit action = the kind, actor = the approver).
- `judge()` in `data.ts` decides every change, when it is asked and again inside the approving commit, with the roles read inside the commit: nobody acts on a role above their own (an Admin: at or below Admin); a new role is never above the requester's; the last active Owner is never frozen, removed or lowered; a role that needs hardware keys is not given to a seat that holds an active synced passkey; and the role of a seat does not change while the seat is in quarantine (the passkey on it may be an issuer's own, and a stop reads the order of the replacements from stop dates that depend on the role).
- One person is enough in two cases: a freeze (it only tightens), and the removal of an invited seat nobody enrolled on. A seat in quarantine does not freeze: a contested Owner seat could freeze the other Owner and then be stopped, and no Owner would be left.
- Freeze: seat `frozen`; its active passkeys `suspended` with no stop-right date; sessions end; its open tickets, the tickets it issued and the requests it made are closed. A passkey of a frozen seat that passes its sign-in check gets 403 `seat_frozen` with who froze it and when (from the audit rows), and no session.
- Unfreeze: the passkeys the freeze suspended are active again; what a stop revoked stays revoked; a passkey replaced before the freeze stays suspended. Remove: passkeys revoked, sessions ended, everything open closed. Role change: sessions end, and what the seat issued or asked for is closed.
- Passkey rule for key roles (`synced_keys_allowed`): an Owner asks. Tightening applies at the approval. Relaxing gets `not_before` = request + 24 h and cannot be approved earlier.
- The 30 day rule: the 72 hour wait replaces the second person only if no seat but the issuer's is, or in the last 30 days was, a key-role seat: active, frozen, removed by two people, or changed in role (dates from the audit rows `seat.remove` and `seat.role`; any role change counts, since it took two key-role people). A key-role re-enrolment therefore never waits: it needs a real second person, or the recovery wallet (M4).
- Limits: 10 new invited seats per issuer per 24 h. A ticket not confirmed 12 days after it was made (its longest life is 11 days) is deleted with its pending passkey when a ticket is next issued. Two live passkeys of one seat never share a label.
- Audit page: `GET /api/audit?before=<seq>`, 50 rows, names resolved at read time, events in words from the one table `EVENTS` (`index.ts`), chain state from `verifyAudit`. `POST /api/audit/export` writes its audit row, then answers the whole log as CSV text inside JSON (a state change is a POST; the page saves the file). Every cell is quoted; a cell a spreadsheet would run gets an apostrophe.

### Wallets and the recovery wallet as built (M4a; owner decisions D1 to D13 of 2026-11-11). On screen: "recovery wallet"
- One signing format, EIP-712. Domain `{ name: "Ophis Business", version: "1", chainId }` of the wallet's pinned chain, no `verifyingContract`. Struct `Link { string action, string site, string organisationId, address wallet, bytes32 nonce, uint64 issuedAt, uint64 expiresAt }`; action `Link this wallet to this organisation`, site the console hostname, times in Unix seconds.
- One verifier, `verify()` in `chain.ts`, for any struct (4b adds its own): the caller builds the typed data from server-held fields; a 65-byte signature with a low `s` that recovers to the wallet is valid with no chain call (never classified by code size: EIP-7702); otherwise `isValidSignature(bytes32,bytes)` must return `0x1626ba7e`, as one 32-byte word, on both endpoints, each of which first answers the pinned `eth_chainId`. An error, a timeout (5 s), an answer over 16 KB or a disagreement is a refusal. `0x` is a Safe message signed onchain.
- Kind (hardened in 4b, H1): `safe` only for a genuine Safe: on both endpoints the runtime code hash is an official Safe proxy's, storage slot 0 holds an official Safe or SafeL2 singleton, and the three reads answer. The allow-list `SAFES` in `chain.ts` (1.3.0 in both deployment flavours, 1.4.1, 1.5.0; all listed on chains 1, 10, 8453 and 42161) comes from the package `@safe-global/safe-deployments` 1.37.67; the proxy code hashes were read on the fork, and `test:chain` proves every line. A wallet whose own key signed is `custodian` when the requester declared it, else `single_key`. Any other contract that passes ERC-1271 is `single_key`, whatever was declared, and nothing it says about owners is kept. "Custodian" is a declaration: the screens say "Custodian wallet (declared, not checked by Ophis)".
- Intent: `seal()` in `passkeys.ts`, base64url(JSON) "." HMAC with the challenge key under the label `wallet-intent`; stateless, 7 days. Fields: organisation, requester seat, chain, wallet (EIP-55), label, custodian flag, 32-byte nonce, issuedAt, expiresAt, site. `POST /api/wallets/intent` writes nothing.
- Proof (`POST /api/proof`, signing host): the MAC first, before any read or chain call; expiry; the signature over typed data rebuilt from the intent and the environment's site. Then one commit, `linkWallet()`: the intent's seat is read again (active Owner or Admin, not in quarantine), the nonce was never accepted (`proof_nonce` is unique and rows stay), the address is not live; it writes the wallet `pending` with a `wallet.link` request of that seat, or `linked` at once where the 30 day rule lets one person stand for two. A pending wallet is live only while its request is open; a reject, a cancel or the 7 days leave a row that the next link sweeps to `unlinked`.
- `wallet.link` and `anchor.set` go through `judge()`, `change()` and `decide()`: the maker is never the checker and every rule is judged again at the approval. Unlink (`wallet.unlink`) and cancel (`anchor.cancel`) are one-person changes through the same `judge()`. The approving commit also reads the requester's quarantine, for every kind of request: what a re-enrolled seat asked for is not applied until its quarantine ends (`requester_quarantine`), and its member can cancel it meanwhile.
- Recovery wallet: an Owner asks on a linked wallet; a different key-role member approves (alone only under the 30 day rule; a single key only there). It is `next` with `anchor_effective_at` = approval + 72 h. "In force" is computed at read (`standing()`); `promote()` writes it down in the next wallet write. No scheduled job. Until then the one in force stays and any Owner cancels (also in quarantine). One change at a time. Never unlinked, in force or waiting.
- Snapshot `{owners, threshold, modules}`: read on both endpoints before the commit, by the handler, at the link and again at the approval as recovery wallet; a Safe that cannot be read is not approved.
- Colour (`colour()`), computed on demand for `GET /api/readiness`: green = genuine Safe, in force, the fallback handler slot (`keccak256("fallback_manager.handler.address")`) holds the official CompatibilityFallbackHandler of the singleton's version, threshold 2 or more and between 1 and the number of owners as bigint, no module, owners and threshold equal to the snapshot. Anything else amber, with its reason; a wallet that still waits says so first, whatever its kind. Readiness (Owner): Owners green (2 or more, each two device-bound keys), amber (one, two keys), red (an Owner with fewer); "Ready" = green Owners, or amber Owners with a green wallet; "Not recoverable" = one Owner and no recovery wallet; else "At risk". The next step is the second key of an Owner (without it that Owner can set nothing), then the recovery wallet, then a second Owner; with a wallet in force and one waiting, the one in force is shown.
- Hosts: a request whose host is neither the console nor the signing page gets 404. The signing page has its own hostname. It serves `/sign/*` (CSP `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; frame-ancestors https://app.safe.global https://*.safe.global`; Inter and the black logo come from its own folder; `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on `manifest.json` only) and `POST /api/proof`, and refuses everything else; the console host refuses those. No cookie is read there. The page takes the request from the fragment or a paste box, shows the fields, and signs by the injected provider (`eth_requestAccounts`, account = wallet, `wallet_switchEthereumChain`, `eth_signTypedData_v4`) or inside Safe (`getInfo` = wallet and chain, `setSafeSettings offChainSigning`, `signTypedMessage`, poll `getOffChainSignature`; "Check again" posts `0x`).
- Not built: cancel of a change of recovery wallet by the old wallet's signature.

### Last-Owner recovery as built (M4b; owner decisions P1 to P9 of 2026-11-11)
- Struct `Proof { string action, string site, string organisationId, address wallet, bytes32 credentialHash, string phrase, bytes32 nonce, uint64 issuedAt, uint64 expiresAt }`, same domain and verifier as `Link`. Three fixed actions (`PROOF_ACTION`): `Replace all Owner passkeys of this organisation`, the same with `, overriding its Owners`, and `Cancel account recovery` (32 zero bytes as hash, no phrase).
- `/recover` (console host, public; the Worker serves the app's one shell under that path). `POST /api/recover/intent`, two calls. First: the options for a new device-bound passkey, for a seat id made there (challenge purpose `recover`, bound to organisation and seat). Second: the registration in, a MAC-signed intent out (label `recover-intent`, 7 days): kind, organisation, seat, the passkey (id, public key, counter, transports), its hash, the three words (`phraseFor` with role `owner`, purpose `recovery` and the intent's nonce), nonce, dates, site. For any well-formed id, nothing is read and nothing written. `POST /api/recover/cancel-intent`: an organisation id and the nonce of a request in, a cancel intent out, nothing read.
- Proof (`POST /api/proof`, by the kind of intent), in this order: MAC; expiry; `countProof()`: 10 per organisation per clock hour, link proofs included, the only write before the commit; the rows (`recoverable()`: organisation active, a recovery wallet in force, no open request, nonce and passkey never seen, 24 hours since the last cancel, no completed recovery that can still be stopped (a second one would complete on top of it and its Owner would survive the stop), a single key only for an organisation of one person; for a cancel: the open request with that nonce); the signature, over typed data built from the intent and the wallet row the server holds (the caller names no wallet or chain, the wallet that signs fills them in on the signing page); the Safe on both endpoints; one commit (`openRecovery()`), which reads the rows again. Until the signature is valid every refusal is the one answer `proof_rejected`.
- That commit writes the request (`waiting`, 72 hours, the Safe as read: `{owners, threshold, modules, handler}`), an `invited` Owner seat "Recovery Owner" holding the pending passkey (no ticket), and one audit row with actor kind `anchor`. Alerts are read from these rows. While the request is open nobody removes that seat or issues a link for it. `ready` is never stored: a request is open while `waiting` and `wait_until` + 7 days is ahead; a row past that is written `lapsed` by the next request. No scheduled job.
- Banner: alert `recovery` in `GET /api/me` (its key holds the session id, so it is shown at every sign-in) with id, nonce, `until`, `lapses`, `override`, `objected` and `may`: `cancel` for an Owner unless it is an override, `object` for an Admin, Approver or Compliance officer while the wait runs and nobody objected. The app keeps it above every page.
- Cancel: `POST /api/recovery/:id/cancel` (Owner, fresh check, role read in the commit, never an override), or the wallet's signature of the cancel sentence with the request's nonce. Completion, an Owner's cancel and an objection find the request through `recoveryRow()`, which reads the organisation's state inside their commit and answers only for an active one; found by its nonce, the wallet's own cancel also closes a request of a frozen organisation. The pending passkey is revoked and the seat removed. The next request opens 24 hours after `closed_at`. Objection: `POST /api/recovery/:id/object` (Admin, Approver, Compliance officer; fresh; while the wait runs): `wait_until` + 72 hours, once.
- Completion: `POST /api/recover/continue/start`, `/finish` (challenge purpose `continue`; the pending passkey finds its request by its seat). Before `wait_until`: 409 with the date. Then `verifyDigest()` on the stored digest and signature, and the Safe on both endpoints. A Safe that cannot be read: 409, nothing changes. A Safe that differs from the snapshot: `lapsed`, whatever its proof says now (an owner who signed may be the one replaced). The same Safe and no valid proof: 409. Otherwise one commit (`completeRecovery()`): seat and passkey active, a session; for every other Owner seat the sessions end, open links and requests close, and the active passkeys are `suspended` with `stop_right_until` = now + 7 days; `organisations.quarantine_until` = now + 7 days; the wallet that signed is the recovery wallet in force, and any change of recovery wallet made meanwhile, waiting or in force, is undone (while a request is open its wallet is not unlinked) and written to `undo`; one audit row, `recovery.complete`.
- Organisation quarantine: the router's quarantine is the later of the seat's and the organisation's, so every `settled` route and every quarantine check of the data module refuses for every seat; `linkWallet()` reads both inside its commit. Re-enrolling an existing seat stays open.
- Stop: `stop/finish` with a passkey of an Owner seat whose stop date is a completed recovery's `closed_at` + 7 days (`recoveryToStop()`; the date alone is not enough, a re-enrolment could give another seat's passkey the same one). A passkey of a removed seat stops nothing. The new seat is removed and its passkeys revoked; the Owner passkeys with that date are active again; the organisation quarantine ends; the recovery wallet is put back from `undo`: the wallet in force, the one that waited and its date, linked again if the new Owner unlinked it (nothing is linked during the quarantine, so its address is free). No seat is frozen. Audit `recovery.stop`.
- The stop rolls back every enrolment made under the recovery (only re-enrolments exist: the quarantine lets nobody join). The audit rows `credential.activate` since the completion are read in order: a link whose issuer, or whose last confirmer (the second person, or the issuer of a Trader or Viewer link), is the new Owner seat or a seat already under the recovery puts its own seat under it. On such a seat the passkey of each such link is revoked, with every passkey its holder added until the next link replaced it. If the seat's last link is such a link, the passkeys that the run of them replaced (they carry the date of its first: activation + 24 or 72 hours) are active again, whether or not their own stop right has ended; after a later link that nobody under the recovery touched, that link's passkey and what it replaced stand as they are. The links the seat issued and the requests it made are closed, and every session a revoked passkey opened ends, so a write of such a seat that is under way finds no session in its commit.
- After 7 days (`gone()` in `data.ts`): an active Owner seat that holds no active passkey and a passkey suspended by a completed recovery whose stop date has passed is removed. Computed where seats are counted or listed (the member list, readiness, the last-Owner count and the seat read of `judge()`, the 30 day rule), and written down by `commit()` with the organisation's next write, with no audit row of its own. Such a seat is no other key-role seat: an organisation of one person links a wallet alone again. A seat re-enrolled since, a frozen one, or one whose passkeys something else replaced stays.
- Override: asked on `/recover`; refused in the commit unless the wallet is green now and an audit row `recovery.cancel` by a seat, or `recovery.stop`, is younger than 30 days. No Owner cancels it. Its completion revokes every passkey of the other Owners (active, suspended or pending) and removes their seats, so no passkey is left that could stop it or bring a seat back. Green at completion follows from the unchanged snapshot.
- Recovery sheet: Security page, for an Owner, from `sheet` in `GET /api/readiness`; `@media print` leaves the sheet alone on the page. Local time travel: `pnpm skip`.

## Routes
A host that is neither the console's nor the signing page's: 404. Every `/api/*` call: `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin` or 403, with one exception, `GET /api/company/callback`: a top-level navigation to a document (`Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate`, `Sec-Fetch-Dest: document`) or 403. Every state change: POST, JSON, `Origin` equal to the configured origin of its host (console or signing page), body under 64 KB. No CORS grant.
The route table is the role table (M3): the role first, then the gates. `twoKeys`: the seat holds two active passkeys, device-bound ones for an Owner; with fewer it can sign in, pass a check and add its second key. `fresh`: a passkey check in the last 5 minutes ("Step-up: yes"). `settled`: not in quarantine.

## Deliberate shortcuts (`ponytail:` in the code)
- The idle clock is written at most once a minute: a session can end up to 60 s early, never late.
- Writes of one organisation are serialised by the audit sequence, with one retry. Many writers at the same instant would need a queue.
- `verifyAudit` reads a whole chain. A removed tail is only detectable with the external hourly anchor, which is not built.
- `scripts/dev.mjs activate` stands in for the first Owner's activation by the recovery wallet (M5). Members no longer need it. It never activates the pending passkey of a recovery request. `skip` stands in for the passing of time: every wait that runs is over.
- Alerts are read from rows of the last 7 days; the server keeps no "seen" mark.
- The audit page verifies the whole chain on every load, and the export answers the whole log at once: fine to about 10,000 rows.
- Relaxing the passkey rule waits before its approval, not after it. Tightening it does not remove synced passkeys already enrolled.
- The actor's role, seat and session are read again inside the commit for every write that depends on them. Quarantine, the two passkeys and the fresh check still come from the session read at the start of the request.
- One person alone can re-enrol a Trader or Viewer seat (M2). Once its quarantine is over, that seat can be promoted by two people like any other: the second person is the control, and is told of the re-enrolment by the 7 day alert only.
- The link is shown as text with a copy button: no QR code (it would need a dependency).
- The counter of proof checks is a fixed window per clock hour (up to 20 across a boundary), and one counter serves link proofs and recovery proofs: whoever holds valid intents of an organisation can use up its hour. Recovery intents are free to anyone until Turnstile is built.
- A wallet's cancel needs the nonce of the request typed in (members see it on the banner, signers on the signing page): the public form reads no row, so it cannot look the nonce up. A request names no person: the new seat is called "Recovery Owner". Completion writes one audit row; no separate `session.create` row.
- A seat once under a stopped recovery stays so until the stop: a link it issues after a colleague re-enrolled it cleanly is rolled back too (the passkey of that clean link itself stands). A rolled-back seat keeps the quarantine date its re-enrolment gave it. A link issued for an Owner seat that is gone is dead when it is used. A recovery wallet that was replaced more than 72 hours before it is needed can do nothing (the cancel of a change by the old wallet's signature is not built). A proof with a bad signature answers `proof_rejected` for every organisation, but later when the organisation has a recovery wallet that could open a request (an endpoint is asked): whoever knows an organisation id can time that. A request of a member that is under way at the instant a recovery completes still commits, as one sent a moment earlier would (the quarantine is read at the start of a request, see above).
- Local endpoints are public ones; production endpoints are decided later. A Safe is read at `latest` on each endpoint: a change in the last block can refuse once. Its first 10 modules are read. The proof is not checked again when the link is approved. A Safe that an endpoint fails to read just after its signature was checked is stored as a single key or custodian wallet, never the other way: unlink it and link it again. `custodian` is the requester's word, shown as such; a single key declared so passes the single-key rule. A Safe upgraded to a singleton that is not in the allow-list is a single key until the list is extended.
- One Worker request per static file, so that the Worker can gate by hostname.
- Company sign-in: the provider's key set is fetched at every sign-in (no cache). A change of the setting has no extra wait (the passkey rule's relaxation has 24 hours). Microsoft's `acct` claim (guest or member) is not read: a guest of the pinned tenant whose verified address is the invited one passes. An organisation with a single key-role person cannot change the setting once verified. Application codes are not listed anywhere once issued.
- Not built yet: other rate limits, Turnstile, email alerts, a passkey check on a change of country or network, the spare key, the production hostname, a sign-in at the real Microsoft or Google.

## Support as built (M6; owner decisions S1 to S10 of 2026-11-11)
- "Select, do not generate": the model (Jev, by TypeSafe AI, USA; `jev-1.14.0`, pinned) only picks ids of the answer base, `src/server/answers.ts`: 25 entries with id, title, one line `about`, a `text` of 80 words at most and the `link` to a console page. What is not built says "Not available yet". No generated text is ever shown. The review checked every text against what is built and corrected seven sentences (a session ends, it does not lock; the 30 day rule; no "at once"; no advice; no legal answers promised).
- `src/server/support.ts`: `secret()`, `clean()`, `select()` (one request by `fetch`, 3 s, answer capped at 16 KB, `redirect: 'manual'`: a redirect is a failure, never another host; the Workers runtime has no `error` mode), `pick()` (the rules), `ask()` (the handler). `src/app/support.tsx`: the page `#/support`. Hostname per environment: `SUPPORT_HOSTS` in `passkeys.ts`. Secret `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` (`.dev.vars`); without it every question is a hand-over.
- `POST /api/support/ask` with `{question}`: any role, no fresh check, no two-key gate, open in quarantine. Answers `{answers: [one or two of {id, title, text, link}]}` or `{handover: true}`; 400 `secret` for a recovery phrase and 400 `invalid` when nothing is left of the question, both before the counter; 429 `too_many`.
- Recovery phrase (G8: refuse before sending, whatever the checksum): 12 or more words of the BIP-39 English list in a row, whatever stands between them. The page says that it was not passed on, and to ask a plain question in other words (a question of 12 list words in a row is refused too). The list ships with viem. Not seen: other languages, 11 words or fewer.
- Cleaning, before anything leaves: one form per character first (NFKC, and characters that take no room are dropped); a word that holds `@` or `://` or a domain name (a letter or digit, a dot, two letters or more: a company domain, a wallet name such as an ENS name) goes; so do `0x` values, hex runs of 16 or more, hex typed in groups (16 or more hex characters in all with a digit among them) and runs of 6 or more digits of any script (also spaced, dashed or in brackets); spaces are single; 500 characters at most. Names cannot be cleaned: the page says "Leave out names and anything secret."
- What leaves, in one request: the cleaned question and, per entry, id, title and the one line. Two questions: a Choice over the ids plus `none`, and a Noul "answered". Never an organisation, a member, a wallet, an id, a session value or the texts.
- Rules: a hand-over unless `answered` is 0.7 or more and the best option is not `none`. Best entry at 0.7 or more: that entry. Below: the best two, never `none`. The page asks "Did this answer your question?"; "No" shows the second once, then the hand-over card ("The help texts did not answer this question."; at once: "No help text was found for this question."), a `mailto:` link with a subject and three empty lines.
- Limit: 20 per seat per clock hour, `countHour()` in `data.ts`, which also counts proofs (`countProof()` is one call of it), kept in the table `proof_checks` under the key `seat:<id>` (ponytail: no table of its own; the same fixed window; a request stamped just before the hour that lands after one of the next hour starts that seat's old hour again). Sessions of one seat share it. No cap per organisation: its seats times 20. It is the only write: no audit row, and the question is neither stored nor logged. A failure (status, timeout, shape, no key) is a hand-over, logged as `support failure` and the name of the error.
- Tests: sections D (11 tests, with the isolation case) and F (the review's attacks, 4 tests) of `tests/worker.test.ts`, the provider a stubbed fetch; the end-to-end flow runs with and without the key. Builder: 55 single mutations, 54 caught at first, the survivor closed. Independent review, 2026-11-11: MUTATIONS_SUPPORT. One Codex pass (it ran out of its 10 minutes and left leads, each checked with a failing test): CODEX_LEADS. semgrep (six rule packs): one finding, not a defect (the `.npmrc` sets pnpm's `minimum-release-age`, the rule looks for npm's key).
- Live check, `pnpm test:jev`, 2026-11-11, twice (builder and review, 32 questions of the prototype): 20 the wanted outcome, 27 with the acceptable ones, the other 5 were hand-overs where an entry exists; no wrong text was shown; median 155 and 166 ms. The review changed texts only, which the model never sees.
- Not built: the design's guards (advice, incident, other organisation), actions, "did you mean", tickets, the audit event per question (decision S4: no row), a cap per organisation, and the tuning of the two thresholds on a labelled set.

## Onboarding as built (M5a; owner decisions A1 to A11 of 2026-11-11)
- Files: `src/server/onboarding.ts` (its handlers and, in its first half, its own rows: the one exception to "SQL lives in data.ts"; every change goes through `commit()`, which `data.ts` now exports), `src/app/onboarding.tsx` (/apply, Onboarding, the KYB record block, the frozen and closed page), `migrations/0006_onboarding.sql` (organisations: `country`, `registration_number`, `review_at`; kyb_records: `reason`; a unique index on `email_domain` among organisations that are not archived), `scripts/dns-stub.mjs`, `scripts/operator.mjs`, `tests/dns.mjs`.
- No open sign-up, and by invitation (G1): `operator invite` makes an application code (MAC label `apply-invite`, 14 days); every call of `/apply` checks it before anything else (the MAC, the expiry, and the one read: no organisation holds its hash), and the organisation stores its SHA-256 unique: a code makes one organisation, once. `/apply` (console host, public, the app's one shell). `POST /api/apply`, two calls, writes nothing: the form (legal name, country, registration number or LEI, family, email domain, a work email at that domain or a subdomain, own name), then a device-bound passkey for a seat id made there (challenge purpose `apply`, bound to seat and domain). No list of mail providers: the DNS proof is the rule.
- `POST /api/apply/check`: the MAC, the expiry, then both resolvers (`RESOLVERS` per environment; DNS-over-HTTPS, JSON form, 5 s, 16 KB each) must return the exact value under exactly that name. G4: an operator freeze ends every session of the organisation in its commit, and a sign-in whose passkey passed its check (an active passkey of an active seat) answers 403 `org_frozen` or `org_closed` with no session: the same plain page.

## Company sign-in as built (owner decisions C1 to C8 of 2026-11-11)
- Passkeys stay the only way to sign in. A company account at Microsoft or Google proves WHO a new passkey belongs to, once, when a member joins and when a member who lost every passkey is re-enrolled. It opens no session and is never asked again.
- Setting (C1): `organisations.company_signin` (`none`, `microsoft`, `google`; NULL until chosen, which works as `none`) and `company_tenant`. An Owner chooses on the Onboarding page and signs in with the own company account (`POST /api/company/start`, then the callback): that pins the tenant (Microsoft: `tid`, and the verified address must be at the proven domain; Google: `hd`, which must equal the proven domain) and links the account to the Owner's seat (`seats.idp`, `idp_tenant`, `idp_subject`). While the organisation is unverified that is one person's change; afterwards it is a request of kind `policy.company` (value `none`, or `<provider> <tenant> <subject>` taken from the verified token; the page is shown provider and tenant only), judged again at the approval, where the asker's account is linked. `POST /api/policy/company` can only ask for `none`. The seed command gives an organisation the domain `northwind.example` and `none`; `seed --company microsoft|google` pins the stand-in's tenant of that domain.
- Invitation (C2): on such an organisation `POST /api/tickets` needs `email`, a work address at the proven domain or a subdomain (`emailAt()`), else 400 `email_required`. The link carries the path (`idp`, `idp_tenant`) and lives 24 hours for every role. `enrol/start` answers `company: {provider, email, confirmed}` and no passkey options until the provider has confirmed; `enrol/finish` refuses with 403 `company_signin_required`, and the commit that stores a passkey reads `idp_at` again. After the sign-in the member creates the passkey; `enrol/finish` answers `{state: pending}` and no words. The inviter sees the address "confirmed by Microsoft" or "by Google" and the key type under "Waiting for confirmation" and approves through the confirm routes: a passkey check bound to the link, no words (what is typed is not read). Key roles: a second key-role person, or the 72 hour wait under the 30 day rule, as built. At the activation the seat takes the account and the address of the link.
- Re-enrolment (C3): the same path. A seat linked to an account of the organisation's provider and tenant needs no address: the account that comes back must be that subject (`account`). A seat that is not linked (it joined with three words, or under another provider or tenant) needs the address again, as an invitation does. An account that is another seat's (not removed) confirms nothing (`in_use`). The stop right and the quarantine are unchanged.
- The path is the organisation's choice (C4): fixed on the link when it is issued, compared with the organisation's setting whenever the link is read (`PATH` in `data.ts`): a link of a setting or tenant that changed since is dead without a write. No field of a request chooses it.
- Protocol (C5, `oidc.ts`): authorization code with PKCE (S256), state and nonce, scope `openid email`. `begin()` in `index.ts` makes the three values and seals them, with what the sign-in is for (the ticket, or organisation, seat and session, and the page to come back to), into the cookie `__Host-company` (label `company-flow`; Secure, HttpOnly, Path=/, SameSite=Lax because the provider sends the browser back cross-site; 10 minutes). The provider gets the state, the nonce and the hash of the verifier.
- `GET /api/company/callback`: the one route that takes a cross-site request. Gate: `Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate` and `Sec-Fetch-Dest: document`. It reads the code and the state from the query and nothing else. Order: the cookie (MAC, age); the state; the exchange at the token endpoint (client secret, PKCE verifier; 5 s, 64 KB, no redirect followed); `verifyIdToken()`: RS256 by the key the header names from the provider's key set, issuer (Microsoft: the constant with the token's own `tid`, a GUID that is not the consumer tenant `9188040d-...`), audience equal to the client id, expiry, the cookie's nonce, a subject, and a verified address: Google `email_verified === true` with an `hd` claim; Microsoft `xms_edov === true` (the plain `email` claim "isn't guaranteed to be correct", Microsoft's optional claims reference, read 2026-11-11). Then, for a link: the tenant equals the link's, and one commit (`confirmCompany()`: link still issued and unconfirmed, the organisation's setting read again, the address equal to the invited one in lower case or the subject equal to the linked one, the account no other seat's); for an Owner: the domain rule, then `change()` with the session and role read inside the commit. The answer is always 303 to `/?company=<word>#/<page>` (for a link: the link again in the fragment) and clears the cookie; the app takes the word out of the address bar and says it in plain words (`CAME` in `ui.tsx`). The session cookie is Strict and does not travel with the callback: nothing there depends on it.
- Local stand-in (C6): `scripts/idp-stub.mjs`, both providers on `http://127.0.0.1:8792`, a page where the tester types the address to sign in as, a token endpoint that checks the code, the client, a secret, the redirect and the PKCE verifier, and a key set made at each start. It shares no code with the Worker. A company domain gets a stable tenant id (`tenantOf`); a personal domain gets the consumer tenant or no `hd`, which the Worker refuses. It sends codes to `http://localhost:8787/api/company/callback` only.
- Tests: section G of `tests/worker.test.ts` (14 tests), the provider a stubbed fetch with real RS256 signatures. Builder, 2026-11-11: 63 single mutations, 59 caught at first; 3 survivors closed by new cases (a link's tenant in `PATH`, the provider in "linked", the setting read again in the confirming commit, proved by a change at the same instant); 1 check deleted (the link's own immutable path read again in the commit). `tests/e2e.mjs` runs the Owner's sign-in on the Onboarding page and one invitation through the stand-in, with a wrong account refused first: 145 checks pass. No mutation was run against the pages or the end-to-end script for this part. No independent review yet.
- Not done, said plainly: no request has ever been made to the real Microsoft or Google endpoints; the app registrations do not exist; whether real tokens carry `xms_edov` as a boolean, and `hd` for every Workspace account, is taken from the documentation, not seen.
