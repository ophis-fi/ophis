# Ophis Business console: architecture

Local only. Milestones 1 (sign-in core), 2 (enrolment and recovery ceremony for members, step-up), 3 (roles, two-person approval, freeze rules, audit log page), 4a (wallets, the signing page, the recovery wallet, readiness), 4b (last-Owner recovery), 5a (registration, with its fixes G0 to G8), 6 (Support), 5b (the sealed declaration, 2026-11-12) Overview and Desk in demo mode (2026-11-12), partner figures: live (2026-11-12) sanctions screening, manual and at no cost (2026-11-13) and M10, the OFAC list as a file (2026-11-13; the last section) are built. The referral share and the flags were removed on 2026-11-13 (owner decision; "Partner figures: live"), and so was the company sign-in at Microsoft or Google ("Company sign-in: removed"). The same day the daily limit of new members went, and an application now names up to 4 more people, whom Ophis approves with the organisation ("People approved with the organisation").
Design sources: `security-design.md`, `recovery.md`, `key-custody.md` (owner research folder). The login identifies people. It never signs a trade.

## Configuration
- `ENVIRONMENT` (wrangler var) selects the constants in `passkeys.ts`: relying-party id, origin, the signing page's origin, and two JSON-RPC endpoints from different operators per chain (1, 10, 8453, 42161). None is ever read from a request.
  Today only `local` exists: `localhost`, `http://localhost:8787`, signing page `http://127.0.0.1:8787`, public endpoints (each answered its `eth_chainId` on 2026-11-11). An unknown value fails every call with 500.
- A production entry is added once the hostname is chosen. The relying-party id is the full hostname, and it is final: passkeys are tied to it.
- Secrets (`.dev.vars`, made by `seed`, never printed): `CHALLENGE_KEY` (HMAC key of the stateless challenges and of every sealed text, 32 characters or more), `OPERATOR_TOKEN`, `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` (Support).
- R2 binding `SEALED` (5b): sealed files, ciphertext only. Local simulation only; the bucket name is a placeholder, and the real bucket must be created in the EU jurisdiction. The sealing recipient is a constant per environment: `SEAL_RECIPIENT` (`.dev.vars`, public, for the fingerprint) and `VITE_SEAL_LOCAL` (`.env.local`, compiled into the console) hold the LOCAL test recipient that `seed` makes with its identity file `.dev.seal-identity` (git-ignored). No other environment has one.
- M10: `SANCTIONS_URL` (a plain variable: where the OFAC list file is; the rule of `INDEXER_URL`; empty: nothing is read; set and not taken: `unavailable`, and the list line is stale). Locally in `.dev.vars`, made by `seed`, pointing at the stand-in.
- Partner figures: `INDEXER_URL` (a plain variable: where the rebate indexer answers; https, or plain http to this machine in `local` only) and `INDEXER_TOKEN` (a secret: the indexer's `BUSINESS_API_TOKEN`). Locally both are in `.dev.vars`, made by `seed`, and point at the stand-in. Without them a partner's Overview says "not connected".
- D1 binding `DB`. The real database must be created with `--jurisdiction=eu`. The id in `wrangler.jsonc` is a placeholder.

Audit chain: `hash = SHA-256(JSON([org_id, seq, at, actor_kind, actor_id, session_id, action, subject_id, detail, country, asn, prev_hash]))`; the first `prev_hash` is 64 zeros.
Every state change goes through `commit()` in `data.ts`: the change and its audit row are one `db.batch()`, one transaction.
`commit()` reads the head, then runs the caller's plan, then writes with the next sequence number. Any other write of the organisation in between takes that number and the batch fails, so what a plan read is still true when it commits. One retry.
Rule for new code: whatever decides a write is read inside the plan. A read made by the handler before `commit()` is stale by then (the review found one: a cancelled ticket came back when its link was used at the same instant).

### The ceremony as built (M2; `recovery.md` sections 1 and 2)
- Ticket: 256-bit token, only in the link fragment and in one response; SHA-256 stored. 10 min for key roles, 24 h for Trader and Viewer. This is the one path of every organisation, whatever its domain and its systems (the company sign-in was removed on 2026-11-13). Owner decisions of 2026-11-13: the issuer confirms after checking with the member, by a channel both trust; nothing is read out or typed. Ophis vets the company; from then on its Owners and Admins answer for who they add. 3 per issuer and seat per 24 h, so a seat may have several open links at once. Issuer: Owner or Admin, never above the own role, never the own seat. The seat decides the kind: `invited` gives an invitation, `active` a re-enrolment.
- After the passkey: `enrol/finish` answers `{state: pending}` and nothing more; the member's page says that the colleague who invited them now confirms it (a seeded first Owner's page names the activate command instead). The issuer's card (`storedAt` of `GET /api/org`) names the member, the role and when the passkey was created, and says to confirm only after checking with the member, by a channel both trust, that they created it.
- Confirmation: the issuer confirms within 60 min of the enrolment, in a request that carries its own passkey check (challenge purpose `confirm`, bound to ticket id and credential hash, 5 minutes); `confirmTicket()` reads the ticket again inside its commit. Nothing is typed, so there are no tries. What protects it: the one-time link and its short life, one passkey per link, the issuer alone confirming with a passkey check of its own within 60 minutes, the second key-role person or the 72 h wait, no invitation while in quarantine, and on a re-enrolment the stop right and the quarantine. An invitation is bound to the person only by the channel the inviter sends the link by and the inviter's own confirmation. The console does not check that the passkey on a link is the member's, nor that the second person confirms the member's passkey and not one on another open link of the same seat. Both rest only on the issuer's check with the member by a channel both trust, and a wrong passkey no longer ends a link: it lapses 60 minutes after its passkey, unless an Owner cancels it first.
- Key roles: the issuer's confirmation writes an `approvals` row (`ticket.confirm`, requester = issuer). A second key-role member (not issuer, not target) confirms with a passkey check within the same 60 min and becomes its checker. Only where the 30 day rule (M3, below) allows it: `not_before` = 72 h, and the pending passkey becomes active at its first sign-in in the 7 days after. No scheduled job. Neither applies to the first passkey of a seat Ophis approved: the issuer's confirmation activates it ("People approved with the organisation").
- Activation: new passkey `active`; on a re-enrolment the seat's active passkeys become `suspended` with `stop_right_until`, and the seat gets `quarantine_until` (24 h Trader and Viewer, 72 h key roles); the seat's sessions end; its other open tickets die.
- Stop: a suspended passkey with a running stop right opens no session. `signin/finish` tells it so (403 `passkey_replaced`) once its check passed; `stop/finish` then revokes the seat's active passkeys, restores the stopping passkey and the ones replaced with or before it, revokes every passkey added after it was replaced, and freezes the seat. A colleague's freeze does not take the stop right away: on a frozen seat the replaced passkey still stops, until the same date, and the stop also revokes what the freeze had suspended (review of M3: otherwise one Owner could re-enrol a seat, freeze it so that the member cannot stop, and keep the key after an unfreeze).
- A seat is frozen by a stop or by a colleague's freeze (M3). Nobody revokes the last active passkey of a seat, so nobody clears the way for a re-enrolment the member could not stop. A freeze closes, in the tables, the open tickets for that seat and the ones it issued; a frozen seat gets no ticket. A ticket is also dead when read if its issuer is not active.
- Only active passkeys count: toward the two a seat needs and toward the 10 it may hold. Replaced and pending ones do not.
- A sign-in with a passkey the member still holds kills every open ticket of that seat (audit detail `ticket_killed`): this is how the target cancels during the wait.
- Alerts are computed at `GET /api/me` from rows of the last 7 days (no table, no email): quarantine, new passkey on the seat, re-enrolment started or completed, a colleague re-enrolled or joined (not shown to the issuer), a frozen seat (who froze it and when), a confirmation or a request waiting for this member, a change two people made (not shown to its approver), and for Owners and Admins a sign-in that closed a re-enrolment ("the issuer is flagged"). The app shows them before the page; the browser remembers what was read.
- Proof, builder, 2026-11-13 (confirmation by a passkey check only): 5 single mutations of the issuer's step, each caught by a test (the binding of the check, the check itself, the ticket read again inside the commit, the quarantine of an invitation, the 60 minutes). A comparison in `confirmTicket()` of the pending passkey with the one the handler saw was deleted: a ticket holds one passkey, so it could not differ. A signature over the earlier Proof struct is refused (B `P1`, E `A6`). Support answers KB-04, KB-14 and KB-22 rewritten; `test:jev` was not run (it needs the network; the `about` line of KB-14 changed). Type check clean, 455 unit tests, end-to-end 235 checks, `test:chain` 89, tour check passed. Review fixes, same day: what protects each flow now, and what the console no longer checks, is said in its bullet (above, `/recover`, override, activation). The Security page and KB-14 say that the console asks the colleague to check, not that it checks. The first Owner's page names no colleague; a cancel's signing page names no passkey; the Onboarding page and the README no longer ask the signers to compare the passkey hash; `nonce` and `attempts` were dropped (0013). The guard of the words also refuses "read out", "read you" and "aloud". 3 single mutations, all caught: the "read out" sentence back, the cancel's label gone, and against `tests/e2e.mjs` the colleague sentence for the first Owner. After both changes and their fixes: type check clean, 451 unit tests, end-to-end 234 checks, `test:chain` 89, tour check passed.

### Roles, two-person changes and freezes as built (M3; owner decision of 2026-11-11)
- Role table: `routes` in `index.ts` is the table. A row holds the roles (or `public`), `fresh`, `twoKeys`, `settled` (refused in quarantine), `second` (the kind of change) and `can` (its sentence). `api()` enforces the role, then the gates. A `second` route has no handler: all the router can do is file its request. `GET /api/org` returns the rows as `rights`, and the page "What each role can do" renders them.
- Request: an `approvals` row (action = kind, subject, payload `{"value"}`, requester, 7 days). Kinds: `seat.unfreeze`, `seat.remove`, `seat.role`, `policy.synced_keys`. One open request per kind and subject. The requester cancels. A different key-role member rejects, or approves after a fresh check: the change is applied in the commit of the approval (audit action = the kind, actor = the approver).
- `judge()` in `data.ts` decides every change, when it is asked and again inside the approving commit, with the roles read inside the commit: nobody acts on a role above their own (an Admin: at or below Admin); a new role is never above the requester's; the last active Owner is never frozen, removed or lowered; a role that needs hardware keys is not given to a seat that holds an active synced passkey; and the role of a seat does not change while the seat is in quarantine (the passkey on it may be an issuer's own, and a stop reads the order of the replacements from stop dates that depend on the role).
- One person is enough in two cases: a freeze (it only tightens), and the removal of an invited seat nobody enrolled on. A seat in quarantine does not freeze: a contested Owner seat could freeze the other Owner and then be stopped, and no Owner would be left.
- Freeze: seat `frozen`; its active passkeys `suspended` with no stop-right date; sessions end; its open tickets, the tickets it issued and the requests it made are closed. A passkey of a frozen seat that passes its sign-in check gets 403 `seat_frozen` with who froze it and when (from the audit rows), and no session.
- Unfreeze: the passkeys the freeze suspended are active again; what a stop revoked stays revoked; a passkey replaced before the freeze stays suspended. Remove: passkeys revoked, sessions ended, everything open closed. Role change: sessions end, and what the seat issued or asked for is closed.
- Passkey rule for key roles (`synced_keys_allowed`): an Owner asks. Tightening applies at the approval. Relaxing gets `not_before` = request + 24 h and cannot be approved earlier.
- The 30 day rule: the 72 hour wait replaces the second person only if no seat but the issuer's is, or in the last 30 days was, a key-role seat: active, frozen, removed by two people, or changed in role (dates from the audit rows `seat.remove` and `seat.role`; any role change counts, since it took two key-role people). A key-role re-enrolment therefore never waits: it needs a real second person, or the recovery wallet (M4).
- Limits: none on new members (owner decision of 2026-11-13: an Owner or Admin adds as many as needed; `MAX_SEATS` and `seat_limit` are gone), and 3 links per issuer and seat per 24 h (M2). A ticket not confirmed 12 days after it was made (its longest life is 11 days) is deleted with its pending passkey when a ticket is next issued. Two live passkeys of one seat never share a label.
- Audit page: `GET /api/audit?before=<seq>`, 50 rows, names resolved at read time, events in words from the one table `EVENTS` (`index.ts`), chain state from `verifyAudit`. An action that leaves `EVENTS` reads "Unlisted event" in a log that already holds it: so far only in local databases, for `ticket.attempt` (a refused confirmation), `screening.flag` (a flag), `ticket.company` and `policy.company` (the company sign-in), all removed on 2026-11-13; 0014 cancels a `policy.company` request still waiting there. Rebuild such a database: delete `.wrangler/state`, then migrate and seed. `POST /api/audit/export` writes its audit row, then answers the whole log as CSV text inside JSON (a state change is a POST; the page saves the file). Every cell is quoted; a cell a spreadsheet would run gets an apostrophe.

### Wallets and the recovery wallet as built (M4a; owner decisions D1 to D13 of 2026-11-11). On screen: "recovery wallet"
- One signing format, EIP-712. Domain `{ name: "Ophis Business", version: "1", chainId }` of the wallet's pinned chain, no `verifyingContract`. Struct `Link { string action, string site, string organisationId, address wallet, bytes32 nonce, uint64 issuedAt, uint64 expiresAt }`; action `Link this wallet to this organisation`, site the console hostname, times in Unix seconds.
- One verifier, `verify()` in `chain.ts`, for any struct (4b adds its own): the caller builds the typed data from server-held fields; a 65-byte signature with a low `s` that recovers to the wallet is valid with no chain call (never classified by code size: EIP-7702); otherwise `isValidSignature(bytes32,bytes)` must return `0x1626ba7e`, as one 32-byte word, on both endpoints, each of which first answers the pinned `eth_chainId`. An error, a timeout (5 s), an answer over 16 KB or a disagreement is a refusal. `0x` is a Safe message signed onchain.
- Kind (hardened in 4b, H1): `safe` only for a genuine Safe: on both endpoints the runtime code hash is an official Safe proxy's, storage slot 0 holds an official Safe or SafeL2 singleton, and the three reads answer. The allow-list `SAFES` in `chain.ts` (1.3.0 in both deployment flavours, 1.4.1, 1.5.0; all listed on chains 1, 10, 8453 and 42161) comes from the package `@safe-global/safe-deployments` 1.37.67; the proxy code hashes were read on the fork, and `test:chain` proves every line. A wallet whose own key signed is `custodian` when the requester declared it, else `single_key`. Any other contract that passes ERC-1271 is `single_key`, whatever was declared, and nothing it says about owners is kept. "Custodian" is a declaration: the screens say "Custodian wallet (declared, not checked by Ophis)".
- Intent: `seal()` in `passkeys.ts`, base64url(JSON) "." HMAC with the challenge key under the label `wallet-intent`; stateless, 7 days. Fields: organisation, requester seat, chain, wallet (EIP-55), label, custodian flag, 32-byte nonce, issuedAt, expiresAt, site. `POST /api/wallets/intent` writes nothing.
- Proof (`POST /api/proof`, signing host): the MAC first, before any read or chain call; expiry; the signature over typed data rebuilt from the intent and the environment's site. Then one commit, `linkWallet()`: the intent's seat is read again (active Owner or Admin, not in quarantine), the nonce was never accepted (`proof_nonce` is unique and rows stay), the address is not live; it writes the wallet `pending` with a `wallet.link` request of that seat, or `linked` at once where the 30 day rule lets one person stand for two. A pending wallet is live only while its request is open; a reject, a cancel or the 7 days leave a row that the next link sweeps to `unlinked`.
- `wallet.link` and `anchor.set` go through `judge()`, `change()` and `decide()`: the maker is never the checker and every rule is judged again at the approval. Unlink (`wallet.unlink`) and cancel (`anchor.cancel`) are one-person changes through the same `judge()`. The approving commit also reads the requester's quarantine, for every kind of request: what a re-enrolled seat asked for is not applied until its quarantine ends (`requester_quarantine`), and its member can cancel it meanwhile.
- Recovery wallet: an Owner asks on a linked wallet; a different key-role member approves (alone only under the 30 day rule; a single key only there). It is `next` with `anchor_effective_at` = approval + 72 h. "In force" is computed at read (`standing()`); `promote()` writes it down in the next wallet write. No scheduled job. Until then the one in force stays and any Owner cancels (also in quarantine). One change at a time. Never unlinked, in force or waiting.
- Snapshot `{owners, threshold, modules}`: read on both endpoints before the commit, by the handler, at the link and again at the approval as recovery wallet; a Safe that cannot be read is not approved.
- Colour (`colour()`), computed on demand for `GET /api/readiness`: green = genuine Safe, in force, the fallback handler slot (`keccak256("fallback_manager.handler.address")`) holds the official CompatibilityFallbackHandler of the singleton's version, threshold 2 or more and between 1 and the number of owners as bigint, no module, owners and threshold equal to the snapshot. Anything else amber, with its reason; a wallet that still waits says so first, whatever its kind. Readiness (Owner): Owners green (2 or more, each two device-bound keys), amber (one, two keys), red (an Owner with fewer); "Ready" = green Owners, or amber Owners with a green wallet; "Not recoverable" = one Owner and no recovery wallet; else "At risk". The next step is the second key of an Owner (without it that Owner can set nothing), then the recovery wallet, then a second Owner; with a wallet in force and one waiting, the one in force is shown.
- Hosts: a request whose host is neither the console nor the signing page gets 404. The signing page has its own hostname. It serves `/sign/*` (CSP `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; frame-ancestors https://app.safe.global https://*.safe.global`; Inter and the black logo come from its own folder; `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on `manifest.json` only) and `POST /api/proof`, and refuses everything else; the console host refuses those. No cookie is read there. The page takes the request from the fragment or a paste box, shows the fields, and signs by the injected provider (`eth_requestAccounts`, account = wallet, `wallet_switchEthereumChain`, `eth_signTypedData_v4`) or inside Safe (`getInfo` = wallet and chain, `setSafeSettings offChainSigning`, `signTypedMessage`, poll `getOffChainSignature`; "Check again" posts `0x`).
- Not built: cancel of a change of recovery wallet by the old wallet's signature.

### Last-Owner recovery as built (M4b; owner decisions P1 to P9 of 2026-11-11)
- Struct `Proof { string action, string site, string organisationId, address wallet, bytes32 credentialHash, bytes32 nonce, uint64 issuedAt, uint64 expiresAt }`, same domain and verifier as `Link`. Three fixed actions (`PROOF_ACTION`): `Replace all Owner passkeys of this organisation`, the same with `, overriding its Owners`, and `Cancel account recovery` (32 zero bytes as hash: it names no passkey). Changed on 2026-11-13 (one field fewer): a signature over the earlier struct does not verify.
- `/recover` (console host, public; the Worker serves the app's one shell under that path). `POST /api/recover/intent`, two calls. First: the options for a new device-bound passkey, for a seat id made there (challenge purpose `recover`, bound to organisation and seat). Second: the registration in, a MAC-signed intent out (label `recover-intent`, 7 days): kind, organisation, seat, the passkey (id, public key, counter, transports), its hash, nonce, dates, site. For any well-formed id, nothing is read and nothing written. `POST /api/recover/cancel-intent`: an organisation id and the nonce of a request in, a cancel intent out, nothing read. What protects a recovery now: the wallet's signature over the Proof, the MAC of the intent, `recoverable()`, 10 proof checks per organisation an hour, a device-bound passkey, the 72 hours with banner, cancel and objection, then the quarantine and the 7 day stop. Nothing binds the passkey named in the signing link to the requester beyond the channel the link came by: /recover shows the requester no hash, so the signers have nothing to compare it with. The pages ask the signers for a link from a colleague by a channel they trust, and for the organisation id of the recovery sheet.
- Proof (`POST /api/proof`, by the kind of intent), in this order: MAC; expiry; `countProof()`: 10 per organisation per clock hour, link proofs included, the only write before the commit; the rows (`recoverable()`: organisation active, a recovery wallet in force, no open request, nonce and passkey never seen, 24 hours since the last cancel, no completed recovery that can still be stopped (a second one would complete on top of it and its Owner would survive the stop), a single key only for an organisation of one person; for a cancel: the open request with that nonce); the signature, over typed data built from the intent and the wallet row the server holds (the caller names no wallet or chain, the wallet that signs fills them in on the signing page); the Safe on both endpoints; one commit (`openRecovery()`), which reads the rows again. Until the signature is valid every refusal is the one answer `proof_rejected`.
- That commit writes the request (`waiting`, 72 hours, the Safe as read: `{owners, threshold, modules, handler}`), an `invited` Owner seat "Recovery Owner" holding the pending passkey (no ticket), and one audit row with actor kind `anchor`. Alerts are read from these rows. While the request is open nobody removes that seat or issues a link for it. `ready` is never stored: a request is open while `waiting` and `wait_until` + 7 days is ahead; a row past that is written `lapsed` by the next request. No scheduled job.
- Banner: alert `recovery` in `GET /api/me` (its key holds the session id, so it is shown at every sign-in) with id, nonce, `until`, `lapses`, `override`, `objected` and `may`: `cancel` for an Owner unless it is an override, `object` for an Admin, Approver or Compliance officer while the wait runs and nobody objected. The app keeps it above every page.
- Cancel: `POST /api/recovery/:id/cancel` (Owner, fresh check, role read in the commit, never an override), or the wallet's signature of the cancel sentence with the request's nonce. Completion, an Owner's cancel and an objection find the request through `recoveryRow()`, which reads the organisation's state inside their commit and answers only for an active one; found by its nonce, the wallet's own cancel also closes a request of a frozen organisation. The pending passkey is revoked and the seat removed. The next request opens 24 hours after `closed_at`. Objection: `POST /api/recovery/:id/object` (Admin, Approver, Compliance officer; fresh; while the wait runs): `wait_until` + 72 hours, once.
- Completion: `POST /api/recover/continue/start`, `/finish` (challenge purpose `continue`; the pending passkey finds its request by its seat). Before `wait_until`: 409 with the date. Then `verifyDigest()` on the stored digest and signature, and the Safe on both endpoints. A Safe that cannot be read: 409, nothing changes. A Safe that differs from the snapshot: `lapsed`, whatever its proof says now (an owner who signed may be the one replaced). The same Safe and no valid proof: 409. Otherwise one commit (`completeRecovery()`): seat and passkey active, a session; for every other Owner seat the sessions end, open links and requests close, and the active passkeys are `suspended` with `stop_right_until` = now + 7 days; `organisations.quarantine_until` = now + 7 days; the wallet that signed is the recovery wallet in force, and any change of recovery wallet made meanwhile, waiting or in force, is undone (while a request is open its wallet is not unlinked) and written to `undo`; one audit row, `recovery.complete`.
- Organisation quarantine: the router's quarantine is the later of the seat's and the organisation's, so every `settled` route and every quarantine check of the data module refuses for every seat; `linkWallet()` reads both inside its commit. Re-enrolling an existing seat stays open.
- Stop: `stop/finish` with a passkey of an Owner seat whose stop date is a completed recovery's `closed_at` + 7 days (`recoveryToStop()`; the date alone is not enough, a re-enrolment could give another seat's passkey the same one). A passkey of a removed seat stops nothing. The new seat is removed and its passkeys revoked; the Owner passkeys with that date are active again; the organisation quarantine ends; the recovery wallet is put back from `undo`: the wallet in force, the one that waited and its date, linked again if the new Owner unlinked it (nothing is linked during the quarantine, so its address is free). No seat is frozen. Audit `recovery.stop`.
- The stop rolls back every enrolment made under the recovery (only re-enrolments exist: the quarantine lets nobody join). The audit rows `credential.activate` since the completion are read in order: a link whose issuer, or whose last confirmer (the second person, or the issuer of a Trader or Viewer link), is the new Owner seat or a seat already under the recovery puts its own seat under it. On such a seat the passkey of each such link is revoked, with every passkey its holder added until the next link replaced it. If the seat's last link is such a link, the passkeys that the run of them replaced (they carry the date of its first: activation + 24 or 72 hours) are active again, whether or not their own stop right has ended; after a later link that nobody under the recovery touched, that link's passkey and what it replaced stand as they are. The links the seat issued and the requests it made are closed, and every session a revoked passkey opened ends, so a write of such a seat that is under way finds no session in its commit.
- After 7 days (`gone()` in `data.ts`): an active Owner seat that holds no active passkey and a passkey suspended by a completed recovery whose stop date has passed is removed. Computed where seats are counted or listed (the member list, readiness, the last-Owner count and the seat read of `judge()`, the 30 day rule), and written down by `commit()` with the organisation's next write, with no audit row of its own. Such a seat is no other key-role seat: an organisation of one person links a wallet alone again. A seat re-enrolled since, a frozen one, or one whose passkeys something else replaced stays.
- Override: asked on `/recover`; refused in the commit unless the wallet is green now and an audit row `recovery.cancel` by a seat, or `recovery.stop`, is younger than 30 days. No Owner cancels it. Its completion revokes every passkey of the other Owners (active, suspended or pending) and removes their seats, so no passkey is left that could stop it or bring a seat back. Green at completion follows from the unchanged snapshot. Here too the console does not check that the passkey in the link is the requester's. No Owner can cancel or stop an override, so the wallet's own cancel is the only stop left: the signers decide together, on a link that came by a channel they trust.
- Recovery sheet: Security page, for an Owner, from `sheet` in `GET /api/readiness`; `@media print` leaves the sheet alone on the page. Local time travel: `pnpm skip`.

## Routes
A host that is neither the console's nor the signing page's: 404. Every `/api/*` call: `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin` or 403. Every state change: POST, JSON, `Origin` equal to the configured origin of its host (console or signing page), body under 64 KB. No CORS grant.

## Deliberate shortcuts (`ponytail:` in the code)
- The idle clock is written at most once a minute: a session can end up to 60 s early, never late.
- Writes of one organisation are serialised by the audit sequence, with one retry. Many writers at the same instant would need a queue.
- `verifyAudit` reads a whole chain. A removed tail is only detectable with the external hourly anchor, which is not built.
- `scripts/dev.mjs activate` stands in for the first Owner's activation by the recovery wallet (M5). Members no longer need it. It never activates the pending passkey of a recovery request. `skip` stands in for the passing of time: every wait that runs is over.
- Alerts are read from rows of the last 7 days; the server keeps no "seen" mark.
- The audit page verifies the whole chain on every load, and the export answers the whole log at once: fine to about 10,000 rows.
- Relaxing the passkey rule waits before its approval, not after it. Tightening it does not remove synced passkeys already enrolled.
- The actor's role, seat and session are read again inside the commit for every write that depends on them. Quarantine, the two passkeys and the fresh check still come from the session read at the start of the request.
- One person alone can re-enrol a Trader or Viewer seat (M2). Once its quarantine is over, that seat can be promoted by two people like any other: the second person is the control, and is told of the re-enrolment by the 7 day alert only.
- The link is shown as text with a copy button: no QR code (it would need a dependency).
- The counter of proof checks is a fixed window per clock hour (up to 20 across a boundary), and one counter serves link proofs and recovery proofs: whoever holds valid intents of an organisation can use up its hour. Recovery intents are free to anyone until Turnstile is built.
- A wallet's cancel needs the nonce of the request typed in (members see it on the banner, signers on the signing page): the public form reads no row, so it cannot look the nonce up. A request names no person: the new seat is called "Recovery Owner". Completion writes one audit row; no separate `session.create` row.
- A seat once under a stopped recovery stays so until the stop: a link it issues after a colleague re-enrolled it cleanly is rolled back too (the passkey of that clean link itself stands). A rolled-back seat keeps the quarantine date its re-enrolment gave it. A link issued for an Owner seat that is gone is dead when it is used. A recovery wallet that was replaced more than 72 hours before it is needed can do nothing (the cancel of a change by the old wallet's signature is not built). A proof with a bad signature answers `proof_rejected` for every organisation, but later when the organisation has a recovery wallet that could open a request (an endpoint is asked): whoever knows an organisation id can time that. A request of a member that is under way at the instant a recovery completes still commits, as one sent a moment earlier would (the quarantine is read at the start of a request, see above).
- Local endpoints are public ones; production endpoints are decided later. A Safe is read at `latest` on each endpoint: a change in the last block can refuse once. Its first 10 modules are read. The proof is not checked again when the link is approved. A Safe that an endpoint fails to read just after its signature was checked is stored as a single key or custodian wallet, never the other way: unlink it and link it again. `custodian` is the requester's word, shown as such; a single key declared so passes the single-key rule. A Safe upgraded to a singleton that is not in the allow-list is a single key until the list is extended.
- One Worker request per static file, so that the Worker can gate by hostname.
- Demo mode: an order is its approval row and nothing else; the blotter reads the 100 newest. Sending an order asks for no second key (the approval does). `seed --demo` and `seed --live` link a "Demo wallet" that no signature proved, locally only; `stale` ages every passkey check, locally only.
- Partner figures: the wallets are asked one read per page view, with no cache; a partner's amounts are the indexer's floating-point numbers, shown and never added up.
- Not built yet: other rate limits, Turnstile, email alerts, a passkey check on a change of country or network, the spare key, the production hostname.

## Sanctions screening: manual, at no cost (2026-11-13)
The operator downloads the official lists, searches them OFFLINE and records the result with its command (README, "Screening, step by step"). Code: `onboarding.ts` (routes and rows), `data.ts` (`LISTED`, `listed()`, `listedWallet()`), `scripts/operator.mjs`, `Screening` in `pages.tsx`; test files in `tests/fixtures/` (invented names and addresses).
- Stored, per organisation only, on a KYB row: the outcome, the lists as one JSON text with sorted keys (code `eu`, in every screening, `un`, `ofac`, each to the SHA-256 of the list file searched), the SHA-256 of that exact text, the date. Global: the loaded address lists. NEVER stored and never sent: a name of a person, what was found, a list file. The command reads a list file on the operator's machine and sends its SHA-256 and, for `addresses`, the addresses in it.
- Routes. Operator: `POST .../organisations/:id/decision` takes `screening: {outcome, lists}`; `POST .../organisations/:id/screening` with `{outcome, lists, nextReview, tier?, source?}`; `POST /api/operator/sanctions` with `{list, sha256, addresses}`; `GET /api/operator/organisations` also answers `reviews`. Client: `GET /api/screening`.
- S2. An approval needs a screening that says `clear` (none: 400 `screening_required`; `review` or `hit`: 409 `screening_not_clear`), stored on its KYB row; `more` and `refuse` store one when given. A screening of an active or frozen organisation (else `wrong_state`) is ONE commit: a new KYB row (name, number and country from the organisation; tier, source, evidence, decision and its date from its newest record; with no record, a local seed, tier and source come from the command and the decision is approved now, else `no_record`), its audit row `kyb.screen` with the outcome as detail, and for `review` or `hit` on an active organisation the freeze with every session ended, as the operator's freeze. `clear` changes no state. The unfreeze is refused (`screening_not_clear`) while the newest record says `review` or `hit`; a record with no screening blocks nothing.
- The invariant: an active organisation never has a newest record that says `review` or `hit`. Test S2e counts such rows over the whole test database, also after a screening and an unfreeze at the same instant, each way round.
- S3. A load replaces one list whole in one batch: 1 to 20,000 distinct addresses in lower case, in chunks of 99 (D1 binds 100 values a statement); an empty load is refused, so a failed parse never empties a list. Then every organisation with a LIVE wallet on ANY loaded list is answered as a hit, and an active one is frozen in a commit of its own (`screening.wallet`, subject the wallet, sessions ended), read again inside that commit. Live (`LISTED` in `data.ts`, owner's review R1): `linked`; `pending` with an open request; or the `pending` wallet an application named, while the organisation is not yet verified. A row whose request was rejected, cancelled or has expired stays `pending` for good and counts for nothing: it freezes nothing and blocks no unfreeze. A listed address is never linked: the link with its proof, the approval of a link, the activation proof and the operator's approval read the lists inside their commit (`wallet_listed`); the stop of a recovery, which is never refused, does not put such a wallet back. The unfreeze is refused (`wallet_listed`) while a live wallet is listed.
- A proven attempt leaves its trace (R2). A link whose proof is valid at a listed address answers `wallet_listed` and, in that commit, writes the row `screening.attempt` (the member's seat, the address as detail) and freezes the organisation with every session ended: the same two facts in the other order (link, then load) freeze it too. The refused proof is kept as a wallet row in state `unlinked`, never live, the subject of that row: its nonce is used, so the same proof (valid for a week, and taken from anyone by the signing page) answers `proof_used` and cannot freeze the organisation again after an unfreeze. For an application (not yet verified, never frozen) the activation proof writes `screening.refused` once per organisation and address. The pages say "It cannot be linked. The attempt is recorded."
- The backstop (R4): the lists are read inside the organisation's commit, but a load is no commit of that organisation. Two triggers of 0012 on `wallets` abort with `wallet_listed`: an INSERT of a `linked` or `pending` row, and an UPDATE that makes a row `linked`, at a listed address. A third (review F1) refuses an organisation that becomes active while a linked wallet of it is listed: an unfreeze at the instant of a load writes `organisations` only and meets no commit of its own. Such an abort is a failed request (500); sent again, it gets the plain refusal and its record. The stop of a recovery puts back a wallet that is still linked (linked to linked meets no trigger) and leaves an unlinked one unlinked when its address is listed (F3).
- What the operator sees (R3): `reviews` also holds every frozen organisation, whatever its dates, and a frozen one says WHY (F6): the newest row of its log among the actions that freeze, `organisation.freeze`, `screening.wallet`, `screening.attempt` and `kyb.screen` with review or hit (action, detail or else the address of its wallet, date), so a clear screening recorded later hides nothing; an active organisation carries none; an organisation under review carries `refused`, the addresses of its `screening.refused` rows. The command prints both.
- A browser gets: the organisation's result only when its newest record says `clear` (the lists by name, 16 hex of the version, the date, the next review); the loaded lists (name, entries, date, 16 hex of the file's hash); each linked wallet with `listed`. No other outcome is sent anywhere: the audit words of `kyb.screen` say "Result: no match" for `clear` and "sanctions screening recorded, organisation frozen" for the rest, and the detail of a row is never sent. The frozen page does not say why.
- NOT done: no list is downloaded by the server, and the Worker calls no outside service; no per-person result and no detail of a hit is kept in the system (it stays offline with the operator); no screening of a trade's counterparty; no check of politically exposed persons; the calendar is a manual chore (`operator list` prints "Reviews due": no screening, or the next review passed or within 14 days). The page states a date and a result, never that anything is compliant; whether the procedure is enough is a question for the lawyer.
- Shortcuts (`ponytail:`) and limits: hits and due reviews are read whole at each call; a freeze that fails after a load leaves the list loaded (the next load or read of the list file freezes it); a frozen organisation cannot unlink a listed wallet itself; a seat in quarantine, or one that may no longer link, that proves a listed address is refused before the lists are read, with no trace; a next review is a real day at most 5 years ahead (F2); a load makes no commit for a hit that is not active (F4); `seed --demo` and `--live` do not read the lists (the trigger refuses a listed wallet); "On a loaded list" on the page lasts only between the two steps of a load and has no test.

## The OFAC list as a file (M10, 2026-11-13)
The onchain oracle the swap used stopped being updated and misses addresses of the official file. The console reads the official list itself, as ONE small file, once a day. The manual load of the section above stays the fallback and the override. Code: `sync()`, `freezeHits()`, `listsOf()` in `onboarding.ts`; `daily()` in `index.ts` (the cron: the read of the file, nothing else; an error is logged in fixed words and answers `unavailable`). The flags (T3) were removed on 2026-11-13 with the referral share: they marked wallets referred to a partner, the only outside addresses the console held, and it reads none any more. With them went the partner sweep of the daily run, the event `screening.flag` and the `flags` of the operator's list.
- The maker, `scripts/ofac-list.mjs`, runs outside the Worker, where the owner decides (recommended: a file in the public repository, updated daily, so git keeps every version). SDN.XML (downloaded, or `--from`) becomes `{list, source, published, made, sha256, count, addresses}`: the publish day, `made` (the time it ran, UTC, to the second), the SHA-256 of the XML, the addresses sorted, by the one rule shared with `operator addresses` (`addressesIn`). It refuses, writing nothing: no `<sdnList`, no `</sdnList>` at the end (a cut download), no publish date or more than one, a `Record_Count` that is not the number of `<sdnEntry>`, no address. The file is NOT signed: whoever can write it can add or leave out an address.
- `sync()`: `SANCTIONS_URL` empty: `not_connected`. Set, by the rule of the indexer's address (else `unavailable`: never silence), then ONE GET, no redirect followed, 5 s, 2 MB, every field checked (list `ofac`; SHA-256; published a real day, not after tomorrow; made a real time, not more than a day ahead; count equal to the addresses, which are as a load takes them). Outcomes: `unavailable` (any failure: nothing changes); `same`; `held` (not loaded, the reason stored); `loaded` (through `replaceList`: atomic, freezes as a manual load does). `same` and `held` write `checked_at`, `made_at` and the reason only over the row they read (`AND sha256 = ?`), so a manual load that lands in between keeps its state. Triggers: the `scheduled` handler (cron `17 3 * * *`) and `POST /api/operator/sanctions/sync`.
- Five rules, and the file never rolls back a manual load. Same: the same SHA-256 AND the same addresses. Older: the file is not strictly newer than the loaded list (`published <=`) and differs; a manual load of an official XML carries its day. Manual: the loaded list was made by hand, with no day; no file replaces it until the operator loads the official SDN.XML by hand (the maker's JSON by hand gives no day, so the override names SDN.XML). Shrunk: the loaded addresses the file lacks are more than a fifth of the loaded entries. Grown: its new addresses are more than a fifth of them. Counts alone decide nothing. A manual load clears `held` and is never held; a first load is never held. After every load and at every `same` or `held` read, `freezeHits()` freezes an active organisation with a listed live wallet, so a freeze that failed is made at the next read. A freeze by the read is the system's (`actorKind` `system`), by a manual load the operator's; both read "A wallet of the organisation is on a loaded sanctions list, organisation frozen".
- The operator sees a feed that stops: `lists` (entries, published, loadedAt, checkedAt, madeAt, held, `stale`). Stale: `SANCTIONS_URL` is set (even to an address that is not taken) and the last good read of `ofac` (same, held or loaded), or the making of the file it read, is missing or 3 days old or more. A maker that stops while the host still serves its last file is stale on the fourth day. The command prints one line per list, with the day the file was made, and a WARNING when held or stale.
- Stand-in: `scripts/list-stub.mjs` (what the maker builds from `tests/fixtures/sdn-test.xml`, made again at each question; modes ok, shrunk and grown (a day newer), older, shape, down), started by `dev`, `test:e2e` and `tour`. In those two the `sync` command is spawned, not run blocking: the stand-in lives in their process.
- Shortcuts (`ponytail:`) and NOT in this build: every loaded `ofac` address is read at each read of the file; no alert is sent (Telegram), no other reader of the list (the Railgun pool, the payouts, the swap backend), no EU or UN address list as a file, no signature on the file; the maker's download was not run from here (no network). A local database made before `made_at` must be rebuilt: delete `.wrangler/state`, then migrate and seed.
